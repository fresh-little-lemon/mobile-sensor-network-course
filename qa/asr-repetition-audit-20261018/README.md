- Recording 13: 01:33:30–01:39:00.152. Recording 14: 00:49:30–00:53:20. Clips are 15–20 seconds. Two clear-English positive controls and one retained prelecture control are separate from anomaly windows.

Correction sidecars recover <|nocaptions|> probabilities only where that actual token appears in stored top-five logits; otherwise unavailable. Later outputs record the corrected token. No-speech scores and RMS are diagnostics only, never proof of silence or a reason to discard audio.

## Findings and editorial decision

- Both clear-English controls produce broadly matching English from both architectures. Recording 13 Whisper misrecognizes packet / I’m done; recording 14 closely matches the lecture opening. Basic loading/input/generation therefore works on clear speech, but this does not validate difficult segments.
- At 13 / 01:33:30–01:33:45 the original long-window filler loop is replaced by empty Qwen short output and capped Georgian-script Whisper output. At 13 / 01:35:15–01:35:30 Qwen guesses a Chinese sentence while Whisper emits capped Georgian-script repetition. The long filler sequence is not independently corroborated.
- At 14 / 00:49:30–00:49:45 Qwen itself remains capped, while Whisper guesses an unrelated Chinese sentence. At 00:49:45–00:50:00 the two models give different Chinese sentences. The original long-window numbers and conversation cannot be certified from these runs.
- Even when the same Qwen architecture repeats an isolated tail word across window sizes, that is not independent corroboration. No new word or speaker attribution is adopted solely on that basis.
- Decision: keep original raw outputs and existing editorial uncertainty; do not insert model-generated repetitions or newly guessed private-looking conversation into revised/bilingual text. This audit establishes unstable recognition, not the exact underlying speech, silence, or a quantitative signal-to-noise ratio. A reliable listening-based check would be needed to resolve the remaining wording.
